# AIP × Glamorous Toolkit — v0.8 contract reference experiment

**Status:** lightweight **pre-v0.9** experiment; Draft 0.11, owner confirmation and GT implementation pending  
**GT baseline:** official v1.1.657, bundled gt4llm v0.7.341  
**Initial AIP source:** public read-only Sokol111 MCP `https://mcp.daip.io/mcp` (frozen pre-wave-1 C1 evidence)

**Business scenario:** A merchant wants to reuse its existing shop catalog to maintain product listings on another marketplace. Most required capabilities—product details, prices, availability, images and change updates—resemble those of the shop. **The deliberate small difference:** after a merchant disables or deletes a product, the external listing must be withdrawn **within 15 minutes**. This is a **hypothetical merchant requirement**, not an existing Sokol111 promise or a real marketplace policy. Orders, checkout and fulfillment are excluded. The Marketplace Connector is proposed, not an observed binding.

**Core promise question:** **Which capabilities have Sokol111 service owners actually promised to existing shop consumers, and can those local offers support the marketplace's slightly stronger 15-minute withdrawal requirement?** The new consumer may need to assume additional responsibility or obtain another agreement; no local owner is presumed to guarantee the deadline. Dependencies only help locate promisers.

**Owner-confirmed Sokol111 capabilities are a deliberate experiment deliverable.** Prepare small candidate declarations in the **relevant service/API owner-controlled repositories**, preferably using existing contract/documentation carriers. Ask the owners to accept, revise or decline **their actual existing offers and limitations**, not to promise a 15-minute deadline merely to produce a match. Record attributable decisions plus source revisions. An agent's interpretation or commit is never confirmation. Sokol111 is currently **read-only** to this GitHub connection; changes must follow an owner-reviewed contribution route. Do not present unreviewed drafts as confirmed.

**[Read the specification](specification.md)** — three increments: (1) candidate promises and actual owner review; (2) small promise-first GT/Lepiter investigation; (3) fair evaluation. AIP v0.8's incomplete behavioral-promise qualification **does not block** owner declarations, the investigation or E1. E2 remains pending/untested until real owner confirmations are available.

**Five consumer questions from [Daniel Kocot's *Integration Beyond the Interface*](https://adorsys.com/en/techradar/integration-beyond-the-interface%20/):** Why and for whom is the capability offered? What may a consumer rely on? Is that use permitted? Who independently promises what? Under which conditions can those promises remain compatible as things change? The investigation concludes with an evidence-grounded **proceed / conditional proceed / defer** decision, not a marketplace implementation design.

**Promise and evidence separation:** Service owners control confirmed Intent; code/contracts/configuration may contain candidates and behavior evidence; the v0.8 `sends`/`receives` catalog is **demo-assumed confirmed**, not actual owner sign-off. The proposed consumer has no observed binding or accepted promise. GT can display separately pinned Sokol111 owner-confirmed declarations even if AIP v0.8 cannot natively qualify them; neither source may be silently presented as an AIP-confirmed behavioral promise.

**Fair evaluation:** Compare source-only agent, GT without AIP, GT + AIP, and direct agent + AIP on equal pinned source/raw evidence. Score correct owner attribution and scope, meaningful reliance/commitment gaps, compatibility conditions, unjustified promises, decision quality, independently verified unexpected insights and owner/engineering effort. **E1** tests the value of AIP-qualified cooperation knowledge for promise investigation; **E2** tests contextual inspection of genuinely owner-confirmed promises. Doctrine H1/H2 require customer validation.
